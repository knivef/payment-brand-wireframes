  procedures."

### The regulatory change that makes this structural

**Verification of Payee (VoP)**, under the EU Instant Payments Regulation
(Regulation (EU) 2024/886), became mandatory on **9 October 2025**. Every PSP
must check the payee's name against the IBAN before executing a SEPA Credit
Transfer or Instant transfer. It binds banks, payment institutions and EMIs
across the euro area, and extends to the non-euro EEA on **1 January 2027**.

Merge states the consequence plainly, quoted: "On 9 October 2025, the European
Union introduced Verification of Payee, requiring banks to ensure that the
payee's name matches the IBAN before processing instant euro transfers.
Mismatches will be flagged and may prevent the transfer," and "pooled accounts
may be rejected if the names do not align."

### The authorisation research, and the question for kvn

**What the law appears to require for this flow.** Two separate permissions,
not one.

1. **Converting crypto to euro is a crypto-asset service.** Exchange of
   crypto-assets for funds requires **MiCA CASP authorisation** from a national
   competent authority. The transitional period ended **1 July 2026**, so
   authorisation is now mandatory across the EU and EEA. A Class 2 exchange
   service carries a capital requirement of **EUR 125,000**, and a CASP
   authorisation passports to all 30 EEA jurisdictions.
2. **Paying the euro out is a payment service, and CASP does not cover it.**
   Quoted: "A platform that receives fiat or e-money tokens from a user, holds
   them, and transmits them somewhere is operating a payment service, which
   requires either a payment institution license or an Electronic Money
   Institution (EMI) license under PSD2, rather than just a CASP
   authorisation." And: "Any CASP facilitating external transfers after March
   2026 must have its own EMI/PI license or use a licensed agent."
3. **Client fiat has a safeguarding rule.** It "must be placed with an EU credit
   institution or a central bank by the end of the next business day", and never
   used for the firm's own account.

**The two structures real providers use.**
- **Direct.** Hold both, usually in one jurisdiction. Lithuania is described as
  "the default pick for crypto fintechs that need both an EMI license and MiCA
  authorisation inside the same jurisdiction." Fipto publicly describes itself
  as "Europe's first dual-licensed stablecoin payment institution" with both.
- **Agent or partner.** Integrate a licensed PI or EMI for the payment leg.
  Quoted: "You can launch faster by integrating a licensed PI/EMI, but you still
  need MiCA for the crypto side, robust outsourcing oversight, and clear SLAs."

**How the honest ones word the footer disclosure.** This research did not find a
good exemplar, which is itself the finding. Of the two providers whose footers
were fetched directly, **BVNK's is a trademark line** naming a company number
and a London address with no regulator and no licence, and **Mercuryo's is
"© 2026 Mercuryo"** with no legal entity at all, despite holding a MiCA CASP
registration. The disclosure that regulated firms are normally expected to carry
names the legal entity, the authorisation held, the competent authority, and a
register reference, and usually lives on a dedicated legal page rather than in
the homepage footer. **Recommend drafting against the register entry, not
against a competitor's footer.**

**Travel Rule handling for this flow.** Regulation (EU) 2023/1113, fully
applicable since **30 December 2024**, requires originator and beneficiary
information on every crypto-asset transfer. The EU went further than FATF:
**there is no de minimis threshold** between CASPs, unlike the US $3,000 or the
FATF EUR 1,000. Transfers from a self-hosted wallet above **EUR 1,000** trigger
additional verification, while information collection applies at every amount.
IVMS 101 is the messaging standard. Practical consequence for this page: a
customer sending from a self-hosted wallet will face verification steps, and
FAQ 8 should say so plainly rather than promise a frictionless deposit.

### The question, drafted rather than the claim

**For kvn, in writing, before the footer line or FAQ 10 can be written:**

1. Does Sepa Mint hold, or has it applied for, **MiCA CASP authorisation**? In
   which member state, and under which competent authority? If applied for, at
   what stage?
2. For the euro payout leg, which is true:
   a. Sepa Mint holds its **own EMI or PI authorisation**, in which state, or
   b. Sepa Mint acts as an **agent of a licensed EMI or PI**, in which case name
   the principal, since an agent must disclose the principal, or
   c. Sepa Mint **partners** with a licensed provider that contracts directly
   with the customer, in which case the customer's counterparty for the payment
   is that provider and the page must say so.
3. Who holds client funds, and where are they safeguarded?
4. **Whose name is actually on the receiving account**, legally? The customer's
   own company as account holder, or the customer's name as a reference on an
   account held by a licensed institution? **These are different things and the
   entire wedge depends on which one is true.**
5. Is Sepa Mint a **direct SEPA scheme participant**, or does it reach SEPA
   through a sponsor? The page must not imply direct participation if it is
   indirect.
6. Who is the **Travel Rule obligated entity** on the incoming crypto leg?

Until 1, 2 and 4 are answered, the footer stays
`[REGULATORY DISCLOSURE LINE, PENDING]` and FAQ 10 stays unwritten.
