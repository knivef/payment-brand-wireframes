Why this exists

The euro leg is where it breaks

The conversion is the easy part. Getting euro into a European bank account, with an originator the bank recognises and a reference it can file, is the part that stalls.

1

The exchange will not pay out

Withdrawal limits, a source-of-funds request on every attempt at scale, and a support queue measured in days. Not a treasury process.

2

The incoming SEPA credit gets questioned

A euro credit from a counterparty your bank has not seen before, with no clear reference, is exactly the kind of thing that gets held pending an explanation.

3

Nobody tells you the rules before you start

You find out what documentation is required after you have already committed the transaction. By then the money is in motion and the clock is running.

Euro questions

What EU businesses ask first.

Will the euro arrive in my own company account?
Yes. Payout is a SEPA transfer to an account in your entity's name, with a settlement reference tying it to the transaction you sent.
My bank questioned the last incoming crypto-related credit. Will this be different?
We tell you before the first transaction what the payment will look like on your statement and what your bank is likely to ask. That is the part most providers skip.
What does it cost?
2.50 percent either direction, plus a $250 monthly maintenance fee. FX is 0.15 percent over the exchange rate, shown separately. No setup fee and no reserve.
What are the ticket limits?
Minimum $1,000, maximum $500,000 per transaction. Larger is possible if arranged in advance.
Can I go the other way, euro into crypto?
Yes, at the same rate, to a wallet you hold. Same account, no second onboarding.
