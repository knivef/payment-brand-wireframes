Why it bounced

Stablecoin payments fail at the last step, not the first

The conversion is rarely the problem. The problem is the euro landing at your own bank under a name it has never seen, and being sent straight back.

1

The payee check is now mandatory

Since 9 October 2025, every SEPA payment in the euro area is checked against the payee's name before it executes. A mismatch gets flagged and can stop the transfer.

2

Pooled accounts fail that check by design

The account most providers pay from runs in the name of a settlement company or a partner bank in another member state. That is a name your bank has never seen and cannot match.

3

A bounce is not instant

A returned transfer takes 1 to 5 business days to come back, travels back through the chain, and arrives as euro rather than what you sent.

The mechanism

Why payments come back, and what stops it

A returned SEPA payment is almost never about the money. It is about the name. Since October 2025 the receiving bank checks the payee name against the IBAN before it executes, and most stablecoin payments infrastructure pays out of a pooled account held in a settlement company's name, which is a name your bank has no reason to recognise. We settle from an account carrying your company's own registered name, so there is nothing to mismatch. Then we attach the origin record, because the second thing your bank asks about euro with a crypto history is where it came from, and a compliance team that already has the answer does not open a case.

What finance leads ask before moving a euro

Including the one we cannot answer yet, left visibly unanswered rather than filled in.

Why did my bank send my euro payment back?
Almost always the name. Since 9 October 2025 the receiving bank checks the payee name against the IBAN before it executes the transfer, and most providers pay out of a pooled account held in a settlement company's or a partner bank's name. Your bank has no reason to recognise that name, so the check fails. The money then takes 1 to 5 business days to travel back through the chain, and it comes back as euro rather than what you originally sent.
Whose name does the money arrive from?
Your own registered company name. The account is dedicated to you rather than a virtual IBAN alias pointing at a pooled balance shared with other customers. That is the single thing that decides whether the payee check passes, and it is the reason this page exists.
What is Verification of Payee and when did it start?
It is a check required under the EU Instant Payments Regulation, mandatory since 9 October 2025, requiring payment providers to verify that the payee's name matches the IBAN before a SEPA Credit Transfer or Instant transfer goes through. Mismatches get flagged and can stop the payment. It applies across the euro area now and extends to the rest of the EEA on 1 January 2027.
Do you pay out at weekends and on bank holidays?
[WEEKEND AND BANK HOLIDAY BEHAVIOUR.] SEPA Instant is designed to run 24 hours a day, every day. Standard SEPA Credit Transfer only moves on business days, which is why a payout started on a Friday evening can sit until Tuesday with providers that do not support Instant. Four of the nine providers we compared do not support it at all.
What happens if SEPA Instant is unavailable for my bank?
[FALLBACK WHEN INSTANT IS UNAVAILABLE.] Not every receiving institution is reachable over SEPA Instant, and where that is the case the payout falls back to standard Credit Transfer with the business-day timing that implies. We tell you which rail your destination supports before you send, not afterwards.
What is your conversion rate, and is there a spread on top?
[RATE], and no. It is one number, and you see the euro figure you will actually receive before you confirm. For context, published headline rates in this category run from 0.1 percent to 3.95 percent, and at least one provider states there is an additional spread on the stablecoin to fiat rate on top of its headline fee.
My bank keeps asking where the crypto came from. What do you send them?
[WHAT THE RECORD CONTAINS.] Every payout carries an origin record, so your bank's compliance team has the answer before it opens a case. This matters because de-risking is rarely about wrongdoing: accounts get restricted because a bank perceives unmanaged risk, and that increasingly affects regulated, transparent companies operating legally within the EU.
Do I need to send from a self-hosted wallet, and does that change anything?
It changes the checks. Under the EU Transfer of Funds Regulation there is no minimum threshold for originator and beneficiary information between regulated providers, unlike the United States or the FATF baseline. Transfers from a self-hosted wallet above 1,000 euro trigger additional verification steps. We would rather you know that before you start than discover it mid-transfer.
Which countries can you settle into?
[SUPPORTED EEA COUNTRIES.] The euro area is the starting scope, because that is where the payee check is already mandatory. The rest of the EEA follows on 1 January 2027.
