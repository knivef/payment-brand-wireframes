### Category patterns

## 3. Positioning

**Category:** in the buyer's words, "a processor that will actually take a store
my size"

**The two positions treated as taken, as instructed:**
1. Non-custodial settlement you own, no reserve, nobody can freeze it.
2. High volume, multi-MID routing, dedicated underwriter, enterprise terms.

Both were confirmed live in the research rather than assumed. Position 2 is
essentially Peptides Payments' "never go dark" redundancy argument and
SeamlessChex's $100k+ targeting. Position 1 appears in the category as
processors that "route around card networks entirely, settling directly to
merchant-controlled wallets with no intermediary who can freeze anything."

**The wedge:** You can be taking orders today at 20k a month, with the money
arriving as fiat in your own business bank account, without an application, a
sales call, a certification bill, or a volume you have to grow into first.

**Mechanism sentence:** Settlement does not require a card acquirer's
permission, so there is no application to pass, no floor to clear and no
certification to buy, and because FiatPay does the offramp rather than handing
you a wallet, what lands is fiat in the bank account you already have.

**Wedge pressure test, run against both siblings explicitly.**

Could sibling 1 put this on its homepage unchanged? No, and the reason is
structural rather than rhetorical. Sibling 1's promise is settlement you own
that nobody can freeze, which is non-custodial by definition and therefore ends
with the merchant holding the asset. The moment it promises to convert and
deposit fiat into the merchant's bank, it is custodial in the middle and it
contradicts its own headline. The fiat leg is the half it cannot claim.

Could sibling 2 put this on its homepage unchanged? No. Its entire filter is
volume and dedicated underwriting. "No application, no minimum, live today at
20k" is the inverse of its qualification model, not a variation on it.

Is it true at 20k a month? Yes, and that is the test that killed the first two
candidates. Anything framed around reserve terms, custody, freezing or routing
is a restatement of the custody argument and was discarded.

**What this is not.** It is deliberately not a custody argument. It does not say
"you own it", "nobody can freeze it" or "no reserve". It says "you are big
enough for us today, and you will not have to become a payments person."

**Enemy:** the qualification process itself. Not a competitor, and not the card
networks. The thing being attacked is being made to pay and wait to find out you
were always below the line.

## 4. Narrative arc

The instruction was to open on the card problem, land on settlement, not open on
offramp and not bury it. The research forced one change to that, described
under the arc.

1. **Hook:** Your processor dropped you, and every replacement quote starts
   above what you do in a month.
2. **Problem:** You are under 100k. This category is built for someone bigger,
   and the ones that will talk to you want money before they will tell you
   whether you qualify. Up to $500 to apply, $50 to $300 to set up, $1,075 a
   year to certify, $250 to $500 to leave.
3. **Why existing options fail:** The floor is real and it is published if you
   look. The closest comparable provider targets $100,000 a month. One quoted
   specialist requires $500,000. A general research-only seller is told he needs
   a proven six-figure monthly history before cards are even discussed. You are
   not being declined for something you did. You are below the line, and nobody
   says so before invoicing you.
4. **The shift:** Cards are a permission. Settlement is not. The question stops
   being how to qualify for a rail that is not open at your size, and becomes
   which rail is open today.
5. **The mechanism:** Crypto to fiat settlement. The customer pays, FiatPay
   converts, and fiat lands in the business bank account you already have. No
   application, no underwriter, no floor, and no wallet for you to run.
6. **Proof:** [OPEN. Nothing is verified. See section 8. The proof section gets
   deleted at build rather than filled, per the house rule.]
7. **The ask:** Start taking orders today. And the honest close on cards, which
   is the open question in section 8: when you do clear the card floor, that is
   the point at which cards become a real conversation, and we will tell you
   when you are there.

**Where cards sit, and the one change to the brief.** Cards open the page as the
problem, exactly as instructed, and they are what the whole first half is about.
Settlement is the answer in beat 4 and 5, so offramp is neither the opening nor
buried; it arrives as the resolution.

What the research will not support is the second half of the original
instruction, that settlement is "the reason our card rail survives where theirs
did not". For this specific buyer, under 100k, research-use-only, no compliance
function, the evidence says there is probably no card rail to survive. That is
requirement 3 and it is answered in full in section 8. Beat 7 therefore treats
cards as a destination the merchant can be told the truth about, rather than a
capability the page implies FiatPay already has. **If FiatPay does have a card
structure that works at this volume, the arc changes and beat 7 becomes a much
stronger close. That is a question for kvn at this gate.**

### Requirement 3, answered in full: are cards obtainable at this volume?

**The honest answer is no, not through the obvious route, and the page must not
imply otherwise.**

What the evidence says:
- A general peptide seller without LegitScript backing "typically can't get card
  processing at all unless they can show 'a proven history of six-figure plus
  monthly processing': and even then, approval is described as tough, not
  routine."
- One payments professional quoted approval as possible only "above $500,000 a
  month in volume."
- "Peptides for 'research purposes' is nearly impossible to get a processing
  account for. Those days are long gone."
- Card networks classify research chemicals under restricted categories, and
  Mastercard's BRAM programme specifically targets the category.
- LegitScript is enforced by Visa and Mastercard under their Internet Pharmacy
  programmes and **applies only to card-based processing**. It is not
  automatically required for every peptide merchant.

**The structures that do work, in order of how well they fit our buyer:**
1. **Crypto to fiat settlement.** No card network involvement, so no LegitScript,
   no underwriting floor, no approval. This is FiatPay's product and it is the
   only one on this list that works at 20k a month on day one.
2. **ACH or bank-to-bank.** Works, no LegitScript required for research-only
   origination, insulated from card network risk updates. The catch is real and
   should be stated on the page rather than hidden: "fewer than 10% of customers
   chose ACH" when offered the choice.
3. **Telehealth plus LegitScript.** A genuinely workable card path, but it needs
   licensed practitioners and prescriptions. Our buyer is a research-chemical
   store with no compliance function, so this is a different business, not a
   different processor.
4. **Specialised high-risk card acquiring.** Real, but the quoted terms were a
   10 percent rolling reserve with T+7 payout in USDT, and the volume floors
   quoted were $100k and $500k a month.

**What this means for the page.** Saying "we get peptide sellers approved for
cards" would be the same promise the category already makes and that its own
deep-dive contradicts. The page should instead do the thing nobody does: tell a
sub-100k merchant, before he spends anything, which rail is actually open to him
at his size, and be the rail that is. If FiatPay adds a card product later, or
has a structure not visible in public research, that changes beat 7 and needs to
come from kvn.
