Why this takes months

You are running seven sales processes to get one fact

Every conversation in this category is with a party that can only give one answer. A decline after three weeks of integration is indistinguishable from a decline on day one, except for the three weeks.

1

Every conversation has one possible answer

A provider that cannot approve you has no incentive to say so quickly, and none at all to tell you who could.

2

The comparison is not published

Not one of the seven providers we track discloses an approval rate, and not one states what happens when a payment declines. Those are the two facts that decide fit.

3

The headline rate is not the cost

Published card rates run from 1.99 to 5.5 percent, and two providers add a separately quantified spread of up to 1.5 and up to 3 percent on top. A 3.5 percent quote can be a 6.5 percent cost.

Why we can say it

Why we can tell you a fiat to crypto payment gateway is wrong for you

Every other party in this market has a stake in the answer. A provider can only recommend itself, which is why none of the seven publishes an approval rate or names who would take the businesses it turns down. A consultancy on a reseller agreement has input into your pricing, which is a commercial interest in where you land. We do not process anything and we do not sign you to our own agreement, so the only useful thing we have is being right about the match. That is also why we tell you plainly how we are paid, in the pricing section rather than buried.

Which providers do you work with?
[PROVIDERS TRACKED, AND WHICH WE CAN ACTUALLY INTRODUCE TO.] Coverage across the market runs from 64 countries to 180 depending on the provider, and payment method support varies just as widely, which is most of the reason matching is worth doing at all.
Do you process our payments or touch our money?
No. We do not process, convert, hold or custody anything at any point. Your commercial and contractual relationship is with the provider we introduce you to, not with us. That is the structural reason we can tell you a provider is wrong for you.
How do you know whether a provider will approve us?
[WHAT WE ACTUALLY TRACK AND HOW.] What we can say about the market generally is that not one of the seven providers we track publishes an approval rate, and not one states what happens when a payment declines, which is exactly why this is difficult to work out from the outside.
What do you need from us to make a match?
What you are building, the markets you operate in, the payment methods you need, and roughly what volume. Markets and volume do most of the work, because together they rule providers in or out before anything else is considered.
