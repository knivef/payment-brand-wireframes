### The thing that actually ends accounts now: VAMP

The Visa Acquirer Monitoring Program changed on **1 April 2026**, and it is the
single most load-bearing fact in this research.

- The merchant **Excessive threshold dropped from 2.2% to 1.5%**, a 32 percent
  tightening in one step.
- The penalty is **$8 per disputed or fraudulent transaction**, with **no
  warning tier**.
- The VAMP ratio is **(TC40 fraud reports + TC15 disputes) divided by total
  settled transactions**. It replaced VDMP and VFMP, so fraud and disputes are
  now one number rather than two.
- Acquirer thresholds were unchanged: Above Standard 0.5%, Excessive 0.7%.
- Published characterisation: "Merchants who were compliant in March may be in
  violation now without changing a thing."

Set against that: **iGaming chargeback rates typically run 2% to 4%**, against
an ecommerce baseline of roughly 0.5% to 1%. A typical iGaming merchant is
structurally above the new Excessive threshold. Nutra continuity offers and
adult rebills sit in the same problem space for different reasons.

The operational levers that actually move the ratio, all named in published
sources:
- **3D Secure.** "3DS shifts liability from you to the issuing bank on
  authenticated transactions. Even when a chargeback occurs, it doesn't count
  against your monitoring ratios if 3DS was used."
- **Pre-dispute alerts.** Verifi CDRN and Ethoca. "You can issue an automatic
  refund within hours, preventing the dispute from counting in your VAMP ratio."
- **Representment.** The directory notes that of 79 providers reviewed, "70
  state a dispute-resolution process" and "28 shield the merchant from
  chargebacks outright", but which party actually files the representment is
  generally not stated.

### Requirement 4: can one underwriting structure serve all four?

**No, and the page must not imply it can.**

- **iGaming needs its own acquirer relationship.** MCC 7995 is manually
  underwritten, requires a gambling licence, and is rejected outright by the
  mainstream PSPs. Gaming-specialist acquirers exist as a separate category for
  this reason.
- **Peptides needs its own, and may not be servable at all at smaller volumes.**
  It sits in a restricted category under Mastercard BRAM with a LegitScript gate,
  and the practical floor reported in the trade press is a six-figure monthly
  history. This is the vertical most likely to be a false promise.
- **Adult and nutra can plausibly pair.** Both are descriptor-driven and
  rebill-driven, both are continuity-exposed, and both are underwritten on the
  quality of the funnel and the descriptor rather than on a licence.

So the honest structure is **two relationships minimum and realistically three**:
one gaming, one for adult plus nutra, and a separate answer for peptides. The
page should present four verticals as four underwriting paths rather than one
product, and the peptides path needs a volume qualifier or it becomes the same
promise the category already fails to keep. **Flagged for kvn at the Stage 1
gate.**

**Wedge pressure test, run as instructed.**

Could this sit unchanged on highriskpay.com or soarpay.com? No. Both pages are
approval claims end to end: "99% approval for high-risk merchants" and
"High-Risk? No Problem!". Neither names VAMP, a dispute ratio, 3D Secure, Verifi,
Ethoca or representment anywhere. To put this sentence on their homepage they
would have to build the operation first, which is the test.

**My first attempt failed and was discarded.** The first wedge I reached for was
"we publish our commercial terms", on the strength of requirement 1 showing that
none of the six publishes anything. It is true and it is a real gap, but it
fails twice. It is a disclosure argument, and disclosure is already the wedge of
the sibling brand Swipe Wire Pay, which publishes its rate and reserve terms up
front. Two brands in one portfolio cannot both lead on transparency. And
disclosure is a page change, not an operation, so any competitor could copy it in
an afternoon. Published terms therefore stay on the page as **supporting
evidence** in the pricing and comparison sections, and never as the headline.

**Sibling separation, stated explicitly.** Swipe Wire Pay wins on what it tells
you before you sign. CardPayz wins on what it does after. Different axis,
deliberately.

**Enemy:** the handover. The moment approval completes, the account manager goes
quiet, and the ratio becomes your problem discovered through a fine.

### Sibling keyword collision, flagged rather than solved

This needs saying plainly. **Swipe Wire Pay's primary keyword is "high risk
payment processors" (720, SD 29), and it serves the same four verticals.**
CardPayz and Swipe Wire Pay are two brands in one repo selling high-risk card
acquiring to iGaming, adult, peptides and nutra. The head phrases differ and the
SERPs are not identical, but they overlap, and if both rank they will compete
with each other.

That is a portfolio decision, not something keyword selection can fix. The
options are to split by geography (Swipe Wire Pay is EU, UK and offshore;
CardPayz is US, UK and EU, so the UK and EU overlap), to split by vertical, or
to accept the overlap because the brands are aimed at different buyers. **Raised
for kvn rather than quietly worked around.**
