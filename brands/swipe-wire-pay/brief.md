### Category patterns

- **What every competitor claims:** high-risk specialisation, fast or instant
  approval, a high approval rate, multi-currency and global coverage, chargeback
  and fraud tooling, a dedicated account manager, and a long integration logo
  grid. All six claim some version of every one of these. None of it belongs in
  an H1, because it differentiates nothing.

- **What nobody claims:** not one of the six states its reserve terms anywhere
  on the homepage. Five of six show no rate at all. The one figure that exists
  in the set, "starting from 1.79%" on High Risk Pay, sits inside an FAQ answer
  rather than on the page. SecureGlobalPay markets "Transparent & flexible
  pricing" as a named benefit and then publishes no number. Nobody states what
  happens to held funds when the relationship ends. That is the white space.

- **What the category gets wrong:** it sells the approval and stays silent on
  what happens to the money afterwards. The buyer's actual risk is not getting
  approved, it is the reserve and the exit. Published sources describe rolling
  reserve policy as "the number-one undisclosed cost driver" in high-risk
  accounts, with merchants "discovering reserve requirements only after
  contractual commitment", typical terms withholding 5 to 15 percent of each
  transaction for up to 180 days. The same sources give the comparison that
  makes the point concrete: "a processor offering 3.5% with a 10% rolling
  reserve costs far more in locked working capital than one at 4.5% with a 5%
  capped reserve."

- **Objections found in the wild:** public buyer complaint sourcing for this
  category is thinner than for mainstream SaaS, and the strongest material sits
  in payments-industry write-ups summarising forum and Reddit threads rather
  than in threads we could quote directly and attribute. Flagging that honestly
  rather than dressing up inference as primary research. What we did find,
  quoted as published:
  - "Most high-risk businesses report having been through at least one processor
    that froze their funds, applied an undisclosed rolling reserve, or shut
    their account with 30 days' notice."
  - Processors described as having funds frozen randomly, "insane fees," and a
    "we'll approve you fast, then surprise-fee trap."
  - "indefinite holds usually indicating the processor wants you to leave"
  - "Two processors quoting the same discount rate can have wildly different
    reserve requirements."
  - On running card and crypto separately: "each processor exposes its own
    transaction format, settlement currency, and reporting cadence", and
    reconciliation is "the under-discussed operational concern."
  - **Action:** before launch, pull ten to fifteen verbatim quotes from
    r/smallbusiness, r/CryptoCurrency, Trustpilot pages for the six competitors
    above, and the high-risk payments Telegram channels kvn already reads. The
    FAQ and the objection handlers should be built from raw buyer phrasing, and
    what we have now is one layer removed from that. Logged as an open item in
    section 8.

## 3. Positioning

**Category:** high-risk merchant account for crypto-native merchants, in the
buyer's words a "processor that will not freeze my money"

**Positioning statement:**
For founders and heads of payments at crypto-native merchants doing 250k+ a
month who have already had an acquirer reserve, freeze or terminate them,
Swipe Wire Pay is the high-risk acquirer that publishes its rate, its reserve
percentage and its release schedule before you apply. Unlike High Risk Pay,
Soar Payments and SecureGlobalPay, we put the reserve terms on the page instead
of in the contract you sign after onboarding.

**The wedge:** You see the reserve percentage, the hold period and the exit
terms before you apply, and card settlement and stablecoin offramp clear to one
balance so you can see exactly how much of your money is locked.

**Mechanism sentence:** Card settlement and the crypto offramp run on the same
ledger rather than through a third-party partner, so the reserve is a visible
line on one balance the merchant can read, instead of a deduction inside an
acquirer's back office that the merchant only detects when a payout lands short.

**Wedge pressure test.** Two competitors could challenge this tomorrow.
PAYCLY already lists "Crypto Settlement" as a service, and published comparisons
describe providers offering "a unified merchant account that supports card
payments alongside cryptocurrency, with both payment types settling to the same
merchant balance." So **"card and crypto in one stack" on its own is not a
wedge** and must not carry the H1. The defensible half is the disclosure. Any
competitor could publish reserve terms tomorrow, but none of the six does, and
the category's economics depend on reserves staying discretionary, which is why
they resist it. This is a commitment wedge, not a technology moat, and it is
only real for as long as Swipe Wire Pay actually honours it. **If kvn cannot
commit to publishing a real rate card and real reserve terms, this positioning
collapses and the whole brief needs rebuilding around a different wedge.**
Flagged as the decision at the research gate.

**Enemy:** the undisclosed rolling reserve. Not a competitor company, a
practice. Naming a practice rather than a rival is what lets the page attack
all six at once without a defamation problem.

## 4. Narrative arc

1. **Hook:** You did not lose money on the rate. You lost it on the reserve you
   were not shown.
2. **Problem:** You are approved, processing, and quietly financing your own
   acquirer. A percentage of every transaction is withheld, the release schedule
   is not in front of you, and you find out the size of it when a payout lands
   short.
3. **Why existing options fail:** Every provider in this category sells approval
   speed and approval rate. Five of the six we tore down show no rate at all,
   and none of them shows a reserve term. One of them markets transparent
   pricing and publishes no number. You cannot compare two offers on the number
   that actually decides the cost.
4. **The shift:** You already take crypto. That means the reserve is no longer
   the only lever you have, because settlement does not have to sit in one place
   and your crypto revenue in another, reconciled by hand at month end.
5. **The mechanism:** Card settlement and stablecoin offramp clear to one
   balance on one ledger. The reserve is a line on that balance with a date
   against it. You can read what is held and when it releases without asking
   anyone.
6. **Proof:** The rate card, the reserve percentage, the release schedule and
   the exit terms are published before you apply. [PROOF BEAT IS CURRENTLY
   CARRIED ENTIRELY BY THE DISCLOSURE COMMITMENT. NO VOLUME, MERCHANT COUNT,
   LICENCE OR TESTIMONIAL IS VERIFIED YET, SO THE PROOF SECTION GETS DELETED AT
   BUILD RATHER THAN FILLED.]
7. **The ask:** Get your rate and reserve terms in writing before you decide.
   Not a contract, not an onboarding call, the actual numbers.
