Why payouts hurt

Paying people is harder than getting paid

Collection has a thousand providers. Paying hundreds of people, in a dozen currencies, at small ticket sizes, from a balance you hold in stablecoin, has almost none.

1

Small payouts cost more than they are worth

A $40 affiliate payment through a bank wire is mostly fee. So it gets batched, delayed, and the affiliate starts promoting someone else.

2

Your treasury is in crypto, your payees are not

Holding stablecoin and paying in euro means converting, moving, reconciling, and doing it again next cycle.

3

One spreadsheet, twelve currencies

Every corridor a different process, a different cut-off time, and a different person chasing you about a payment that did not land.

Payout questions

What comes up when people scope a run.

What does a payout cost?
One percent, or thirty dollars, whichever is higher. On a $40 payout that floor dominates, so very small payouts are usually better batched per recipient. We will tell you that during scoping.
What is the smallest payout you can send?
$25. The largest single payout is $200,000.
How fast do recipients get paid?
Instant where the corridor supports an instant payment scheme. Otherwise it follows the local rail, typically same or next business day.
Can one run contain several currencies?
Yes. Upload the run once, recipients get paid in their own currency.
Do I have to hold crypto?
You fund the balance in crypto, yes. That is what makes the small-ticket economics work. The recipients never touch crypto.
What happens if a row is wrong?
Validation runs before anything moves, so a bad row is rejected rather than failing the batch. You are only charged for payouts that actually leave.
What does it cost to set up?
$500 one time and $250 per month. Both are on the rate card above.
