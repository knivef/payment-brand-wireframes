Why pre-screening

A declined application is not free

In this vertical, applications are reviewed by humans who have seen a thousand stores like yours. The things that get you declined are almost always visible on your own website before you ever file.

1

Declines follow you

A pattern of applications and declines across acquirers is itself a signal, and the next underwriter can see more of it than you think.

2

The problem is usually the site, not the business

Claims language, missing terms, an unclear refund policy, a research-use disclaimer that is not where it needs to be. All fixable, all fatal if left.

3

Nobody tells you why you failed

Declines come back as a one line answer. You are left guessing which of a dozen things to change before the next attempt.

The screen

What we actually check

Your storefront

Read the way an underwriter reads it, not the way a customer does. Most declines in this vertical are decided here.

  • Claims and indication language on product pages
  • Research use disclaimers, placement and wording
  • Terms, refund and shipping policies
  • Contact details, entity name and jurisdiction visibility

Your file

The pack an acquirer will ask for, checked for the gaps that cause a resubmission rather than a decline.

  • Entity documents and ownership structure
  • Processing history and chargeback ratios where they exist
  • Prior terminations, and how to present them
  • Which acquirer profile you realistically fit

Pre-screening questions

What merchants ask before sending a URL.

Is Pure Labs a payment processor?
No. We pre-screen and vet, then introduce you if you want that. The processing relationship is with an acquirer, not with us.
What does the pre-screen cost?
The initial screen is free. [CONFIRM: pricing for the full vetting and introduction service before launch.]
Will you file an application for me?
Only if you tell us to, in writing. The default is that you get the list and decide what to do with it.
I have been terminated before. Is that fatal?
Usually not. Hiding it is far worse than disclosing it, because the next underwriter will find it and the omission becomes the problem.
What is the most common thing you find?
Claims language on product pages and a research use disclaimer that is either missing or in the wrong place. Both are cheap to fix and both get applications declined.
How long does the screen take?
One business day for the written list on a standard store.
Do you share my details with acquirers?
Not unless you ask us to introduce you. The screen itself is between you and us.
