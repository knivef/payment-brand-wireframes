### Why "global" is not available, and what to write instead

This still governs the page, because the coverage the buyer cares about belongs
to the providers we introduce, and it is uneven.

- **US:** FinCEN MSB registration federally, **plus** separate state money
  transmitter licences. MoonPay holds MTLs in 47 states plus a NY BitLicense,
  and a BitLicense alone takes up to two years.
- **EU:** MiCA CASP authorisation, passporting across 27 member states.
  Transitional window closed 1 July 2026.
- **UK:** FCA registration, with the FSMA (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026
  authorisation window open **September 2026 to February 2027**.
- **Not reachable at all:** sanctioned jurisdictions (Iran, North Korea, Russia,
  Syria), national bans (China, Nepal), partial restrictions (Bolivia, Iraq,
  Morocco, Tunisia, Pakistan, Qatar), and Hawaii within the US.

"Only well-funded providers can maintain the global licensing footprint that
wallets and exchanges demand", and "where none do, the buy button disappears."

**How the honest ones word coverage, quoted.** Nobody credible writes "global".
Onramper writes "coverage in 190+ countries". MoonPay states a licence count, 47
US states plus the BitLicense. Ramp Network publishes a page titled "Which
countries and US states are unsupported". **Onrampr should describe provider
coverage as a range with named exclusions, never as its own global footprint,
and every figure stays bracketed until kvn supplies it.**

## 3. Positioning

**Category:** in the buyer's words, "which one of these will actually take us"

### Stage 1B: the wedge

The instruction was to find the wedge in the gap between starting and arriving.
Under the introducer model that gap moves earlier: it is the gap between a
business deciding it needs fiat to crypto and a provider actually approving it.

**The wedge:** We do not process anything, so we are the only party in this
market with no reason to recommend the wrong provider.

**Mechanism sentence:** Onrampr holds the comparison the providers do not
publish, coverage by country, payment methods, KYC tiers, and the real cost
including the spread that sits under the headline rate, and matches a business
to the providers that will approve it before it spends four weeks finding out
that one will not.

**Pressure test, run properly.** Could any of the seven providers run this
sentence unchanged? **No, and structurally not ever.** A provider recommending a
competitor is sending its own revenue away, which is why none of them publishes
a pass rate or names who would approve the businesses it declines. This is the
strongest structural position of any wedge in this repo so far, because the
incumbents are not merely failing to say it, they are *unable* to say it.

Could Onramper? Closer, and this is the honest complication. It aggregates and
routes, so it is provider-neutral in a technical sense. But it sells an
integration to a team that has already decided to embed an onramp, not a
provider decision to a business still choosing. And the naming collision makes
that adjacency uncomfortable rather than merely competitive.

Could a payments consultancy or ISO? Partly, but the ISO model involves "signing
merchants under their own agreements, often with input into pricing", which is a
commercial interest in the outcome. **The claim is only true for a pure
introducer, which is what Onrampr is.** That makes the model the moat, and it
also makes the model something the page has to state plainly rather than hide.

**Components, and which are contested:**

| Component | Status |
|---|---|
| No stake in which provider wins | **Uncontested by the seven providers, structurally** |
| Holding the coverage, method and KYC comparison | **Partly contested** by published comparison content, which is free but static and undated |
| Knowing who approves whom | **Uncontested.** Nobody publishes a pass rate |
| Making the introduction rather than selling a tool | **Contested** by consultancies and ISOs, with the conflict-of-interest difference above |

**Enemy:** the four-week decline. Not a provider, and deliberately not the
providers as a group, because we introduce businesses to them and the page
cannot insult the supply.

### Sibling separation

FiatPay and Sepa Mint sell crypto to fiat. Onrampr is the reverse direction and
**must not be written as their inverse.** No cross-reference, no "the other
half" framing, no reuse of their argument shapes. There is a cleaner separation
available now than at Stage 0B: those two are operators and Onrampr is an
introducer, so it is a different kind of business, not a mirrored one.

## 4. Narrative arc

1. **Hook:** Seven providers, and not one of them will tell you whether it can
   approve you.
2. **Problem:** You find out by applying. Each conversation is with a party that
   can only give one answer, and a decline after three weeks of integration is

5. **The mechanism:** We do not process anything. We keep the coverage, methods,
   KYC tiers and real costs current, match your business to the providers that
   will approve it, and make the introduction.
6. **Proof:** [OPEN. Nothing verified. Proof section deleted at build rather

7. **The ask:** Tell us what you need to do and where. We will tell you which

### The compliance research, reframed for an introducer

**The model changes the question.** Because Onrampr does not handle funds,
convert assets or provide a crypto-asset service, the registrations researched
earlier are most likely **not applicable** rather than merely unconfirmed. In a
referral model you "point merchants to a payments provider and collect a
commission when they sign up, with no underwriting, support tickets, or
compliance exposure required."

**That is not the same as no obligations, and the page must not overclaim in the
other direction either.** Three things remain live:

1. **The providers will diligence us.** Firms working with unregulated
   introducers are expected to "have a clear understanding of what that entity
   is relying on if they believe they do not need to be authorised", and
   regulators expect "due diligence showing how you satisfied yourself the
   introducer had appropriate controls in place." Our credibility to the
   provider is a commercial precondition, not a formality.
2. **Where the line sits between introducing and advising.** An introduction is
   generally lighter than a recommendation. The page currently says we tell a
   business which providers fit, which is closer to advice. **Whether that is
   introducing or advising in any given market is a legal question, not a copy
   question.**
3. **Commission disclosure.** If Onrampr is paid by the provider, and especially
   if the amount varies by provider, that is material to the independence claim
   which is the entire wedge.

**How the honest ones word a disclosure, quoted.** The best exemplar found is
MoonPay's, which is a provider rather than an introducer but shows the right
shape: **"MoonPay USA LLC is a registered money service business (NMLS ID:
2071245)."** A named legal entity, the specific registration, a verifiable
registry identifier, and scoped to one region rather than a blanket claim. An
introducer's version states the entity, states plainly that it is not authorised
and does not handle funds, and names what it does instead.

**Travel Rule and sanctions screening.** These fall on the providers rather than
on Onrampr, because they attach to whoever moves the value. Recorded for the
brief because the buyer will ask about them: sanctions screening is strict
liability, "under OFAC there is no requirement that you intended to transact
with a blocked party", and the OFAC SDN list now includes crypto wallet
addresses. The EU Transfer of Funds Regulation applies to all transfers with no
de minimis; the US FinCEN threshold is $3,000. Self-hosted wallet transfers above
roughly EUR 1,000 may require proof of ownership.

### The questions, drafted rather than the claims

**For kvn, in writing, before the footer line, FAQ 9 or the pricing section can
be written:**

1. **How is Onrampr paid?** By the provider, by the business, or both? If by the
   provider, is it a flat fee, a share of the provider's revenue, or something
   that varies between providers?
2. **Does the amount vary by provider?** If it does, the independence claim in
   the USP needs rewording, because the structural argument depends on having no
   preference.
3. Is Onrampr a **pure introducer**, or does it sign businesses under its own
   agreement or have input into their pricing, which would make it an ISO?
4. Which **legal entity** operates Onrampr, in which jurisdiction?
5. Has anyone taken **legal advice** on whether the activity is introducing or
   advising, and in which markets? Different answers in the UK, EU and US.
6. **Which providers** can Onrampr actually introduce to today, and is there a
   written arrangement with each?
7. What does Onrampr **actually track** about each provider, how is it kept
   current, and who maintains it?

Until 1, 2 and 3 are answered, the footer stays
`[REGULATORY DISCLOSURE LINE, PENDING]`, FAQ 1 and FAQ 9 stay unwritten, and the
pricing section stays bracketed.
